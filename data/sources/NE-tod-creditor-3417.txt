Sec. 76-3417. Liability for creditor claims and statutory allowances
(a) "If other assets of the estate of the transferor are insufficient to pay all claims against the transferor's estate, statutory allowances to the transferor's surviving spouse and children, and the expenses of administration, a transfer under the Nebraska Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act subjects the beneficiary to personal liability" as outlined in the statute.
(b)(1) A beneficiary receiving property through a transfer on death deed becomes liable to account to the personal representative for a proportionate share of the fair market value of the equity received, with value determined as of the transferor's death date.
(b)(2) Proceedings to assert liability cannot commence unless the personal representative receives a written demand from the surviving spouse, a creditor, a child, or someone acting for a child. The proceeding must begin within one year after the transferor's death.
(c) A beneficiary may join other beneficiaries of transfer on death deeds or related assets in the proceeding.
(d) Assets recovered by the personal representative shall be administered as part of the transferor's estate.
(e) The statute does not limit creditors' rights under other state laws.
